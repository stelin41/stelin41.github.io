Why learning Machine Learning on your own is so hard?

2023-01-07
#tip  #machine learning  #data science  #artificial intelligence  #algorithms  #math 

If you’re like me, you started (and never finished) countless projects about many tech-related topics that you found interesting. If one of such topics were AI/ML, unless you are familiar with data science, I’m really sure that at some point you became completly lost or confused. Anything you try doesn’t work. So, what are you missing? (spoiler: it’s not just math)

Back-story⌗

First of all, you must know that right now I’m just a first-year college student and I barely grasped the surface of the AI world, so take everything I say with a grain of salt. With that said, I do think that I’m the right person to explain this. Not because I’m good at data science, but rather the oposite. I miserably commited all possible mistakes which you can stumble upon while going through the AI jungle, and it took me a long time to realize what I was doing wrong.

The first step you take will be on a landmine⌗

Like any CS enthusiast, google is the best friend when facing against an unknown topic, so the first thing that I (and probably you) did was googling how AI works. First mistake. This is like someone trying to become a hacker while having no idea about networking/programming/linux. I didn’t understand a thing, because all I had was high school level math knowledge. AI is just math build upon math. Therefore, the first step I should have taken was doing lots of math.

Ok, that’s the moment to take a step back and forget about z3, learn about set theory, number theory, algebra, calculus, combinatory, stadistics, probability theory, logic, graph theory, and so on. Now I can finally learn about neural networks and machine learning. This is it, right? All we needed was a strong math foundation. You may be thinking “that’s obvious, duh”.

Well yes, but actually no.

Important considerations⌗

You must understand that data scientists are like artisans. Artisans use different tools to work with different materials. Data scientists use mathematical tools to work with data. The more tools they know how to use, the more chances they have to reach their goal and the better the results will be. One of those tools is machine learning. You may be surprised that, in most cases, using neural networks is a really bad approach. In fact, I recommend avoiding them as much as you can, since most other classical tools are way easier to use and they will probably archieve better results with much less resources.

In the case that what you are trying to archieve is too hard or not possible through common methods, that’s the right moment to consider using neural networks. If so, can you finally do ML? No. This is the exact mistake I was talking about two paragraps up.

What nobody had ever told you⌗

Now that you have the knowledge required to understand the AI toolset, and learned how it works, you will try to have fun with it. However, things won’t turn as you initially thought. Most of your attempts will end up in failure. Why? Because you must understand not only the tools, but also what you are working with: data. I can’t emphasize enough how important that is. Doctors study for many years, and what they usually spend more time studing aren’t treatments for the diseases, but rather, the human body. That’s the thinking pattern you’re probably missing. A good data scientist or AI enthusiast must understand really well the data they’re working with, in a very special way.

Let me show you what I mean with some examples:

I once tried to develop an AI capable to predict the stock market. That’s a really dumb idea. Since all I had was the price history, it’s safe to consider that stock prices are random. You can’t predict something random.

A year and a half ago, I wrote an [application] that’s capable to detect laser dots. Initially I thought that using a neural network would have been a good solution, but that would make it much harder to develop. Instead, after a lot of playing with a video I recorded, I realized that the camera is literally a light sensor. So all I needed to do was filtering the brightest pixels. If I understood that from the beggining, I would have afforded a lot of time.

The rabbit hole of guessing⌗

Data science is huge⌗

At this point, every time you find a new challenge, you may want to use a neural network. Deep in your heart, you still believe that neural networks can do anything. But I must remember you that’s not true (at least, for now). That’s what it means “we don’t have an AGI yet”. This is something obvious too, but programmers, and specially hackers, are tempted to keep “guessing” everything out as they always did. There’s nothing wrong with that, in fact that’s really good approach to take on IT since there’s too much to learn, and if you do something wrong in most cases it’s easy to figure out why.

However, data science and mainly machine learning, is not like most other IT topics. Guessing won’t work.
